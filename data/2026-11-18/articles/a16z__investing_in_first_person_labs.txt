For personal agents to become useful in everyday life, they need a total understanding of the person they’re working for. Building that context, keeping it current, and making it available across applications is a hard systems problem. It also creates something extraordinarily sensitive: a model of your relationships, priorities, habits, and intentions.  Whoever controls that model will have considerable influence over what your agents do for you. We believe you should be in total control of it.
That’s why we’re investing in
First Person Labs
. Its product,
Second
, builds a continuously updated model of you that can serve context to the agents you choose. Your understanding of the world can accumulate across tools instead of getting stranded inside each one.
The architecture matters. Second’s background engine runs on your device by default. Your model is inspectable and portable. You choose the intelligence that powers it and control what other applications and agents can access. These choices give you practical control over an increasingly valuable part of your digital life.
Ken Liu and Erik Chi bring exactly the combination this company needs. Ken’s research at Stanford and Google DeepMind focused on AI and data privacy, including what models remember and how that information can be protected. Erik’s work at Michigan focused on network security, privacy, and censorship resistance. Together, they built the Open Anonymity Project to give people more private access to AI. They understand both how AI learns about people and how to build systems that keep people in control. First Person Labs is building the infrastructure to make that possible, and we’re excited to back the team.
