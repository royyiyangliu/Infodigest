The data comes from an online Newzoo survey conducted in August 2026. Newzoo surveyed 6,166 players aged 18-55 in the US, the UK, Germany, Poland, Brazil, and Saudi Arabia. Only players who play on PC or current-generation consoles at least once a month, and who bought a AAA/AA game or played one through a subscription in the past year, were included.
Audience and Spending
60% of respondents play on both console and PC, 29% on console only, and 11% on PC only. About two-thirds of players are male, and the average age is around 33.

The largest share of PC-only players is in Poland (27%). Saudi Arabia (42%) and the UK (37%) have the highest shares of console-only players.
89% of respondents play on console, and 71% on PC. PlayStation 5 is the most popular console (68%), followed by Nintendo Switch or Switch 2 (41%), and Xbox Series X|S (30%).
On average, a player in the sample spends $368 a year on games. Of that, 46% goes to buying games, 32% to subscriptions, and 21% to in-game purchases (add-ons, currency, battle passes, etc.).
Players in the US ($425 a year) and the UK ($422 a year) spend the most. In Poland and Brazil, it's $245 a year each; in Saudi Arabia, it's $256 a year. In Germany, players spend more on buying games (51%). Germans and Polish gamers spend less on in-game purchases than others (17% and 16%).
Those who play on both console and PC spend the most ($410 a year). Console-only players spend about $306 a year on games, and PC-only players $269 a year.
Over the past year, respondents played 13.3 games on average. The US had the most (14.1), and Germany the fewest (11.0).
When asked what they played, players mostly named long-running franchises like Call of Duty, GTA, Fortnite, and Minecraft. Newzoo points out that back in 2023, 61% of all PC and console playtime went to games more than six years old.

38% of the games respondents played over the past year were F2P. 43% were B2P (32% bought digitally, 11% on physical disks), and another 19% were accessed through a subscription.
Differences between countries are small. F2P play is slightly more common in Saudi Arabia (42%).
Players buy digital copies for instant access after purchase (42%), access to their library from anywhere (38%), and because they can't be lost or damaged (33%).
Reasons for buying physical copies include playing without a stable internet connection (26%), wanting to own something physical (24%), and saving storage space on the device (24%).
❗️The storage space point is questionable, of course. On PlayStation (my main gaming device), even a disc game gets installed on the internal drive.
Prices and Discounts
73% of players think most games are too expensive at launch. The same share say they wait for discounts more often now, and 71% say they've become more careful about spending on games. Players in Saudi Arabia, Poland, and Brazil are the most likely to call games too expensive (79% each).
At the same time, 69% think games give more for the money than TV, movies, and music. 62% agree that game prices reflect rising development costs.
Newzoo notes that many players buy cheaper games from their wishlists or play F2P titles while waiting for new releases to go on sale. 57% say they have so many games on their wishlists that they rarely pay full price. 52% spend less because there are so many F2P titles.
By players' own estimates, over the past three years, they bought 42% of games before release or in the release month (17% pre-orders, 25% in the release month). The remaining 58% were bought later, and 18% of all games were bought more than a year after release.
Players in the US buy earliest: 46% of games before release or in the release month. Players in Poland wait the longest (68% of purchases after the release month).
PC-only players buy 70% of their games after the release month.

The main reason to delay a purchase is waiting for a discount (45%). Next come waiting for bugs to be fixed (37%) and for player reviews (36%).
Players buy at launch to play right after release (48%). That’s the primary reason. Pre-order discounts are second (37%), and wanting to play at the same time as everyone else is third (36%).
78% of players search for discounts regularly or sometimes (45% regularly). The share is highest in Brazil (84%) and Saudi Arabia (83%), and lowest in Germany (74%).
Players mostly learn about discounts from official storefronts (40%), social media (34%), and YouTube (33%). 17% mention reseller and marketplace sites.
On average, players are willing to wait 4.9 months for a game discount to become interesting. The most common answer is 3-4 months (28%). Players in Poland (6.6 months) and Germany (5.8 months) are willing to wait the longest, while those in Brazil (4.4 months) and the US (4.6 months) wait the least.
A 13% discount has almost no effect: the game still feels expensive. At 29% off, the price starts to feel reasonable, and at 45% off, it feels like a good deal. A 70% discount, though, already raises doubts about the game's quality.
In the US, the price feels reasonable at 28% off and like a good deal at 43% off. PC-only players expect more generous discounts.
Over the next 2-3 years, 42% of players plan to play more on PC and console (7% plan to play less). 39% expect to spend more on premium games, and 38% to use subscriptions more. But only 33% plan to buy more games at full price (15% plan to buy fewer games at full price).
❗️Newzoo itself notes that survey respondents more often expect engagement to rise than to fall, so expectations are usually more optimistic than reality.
Subscriptions
Game Pass and PS Plus (Extra or Premium) are each used by 38% of respondents, EA Play by 28%, and Ubisoft+ by 14%. Among console-only players, PS Plus leads (38% vs. 22% for Game Pass).
80% of subscribers say a subscription lets them try genres and games they don't usually buy. 79% like the discounts and promotions, and 77% like the events and rewards.
54% of subscribers say they've spent more on games overall since subscribing. Newzoo suggests this includes both the subscription fee itself and in-game purchases.
That said, 42% of subscribers feel the subscription library is outdated or repetitive.
44% of subscription users have started buying fewer games (13% significantly fewer). 32%, on the other hand, buy more, and 24% report no change.
Subscriptions reduce purchases the most in Poland: 49% buy fewer games, and only 21% buy more. In Saudi Arabia, by contrast, more subscribers buy more games (42%) than fewer (38%). Among PC-only players, 54% buy fewer games.
Players buy fewer games mainly because they have enough games in the subscription (41%), and because they try games on the subscription before buying (34%). Only 22% say buying games has become unnecessary altogether.
Those who buy more say they discover more games (38%) and are more inclined to buy after trying a game on the subscription (38%).
Players are more likely to make in-game purchases in subscription games: 32-36% of subscribers are more likely to buy add-ons or cosmetics in a subscription game, compared with only 15-19% in a game they own. Newzoo links this to players saving on the game itself.

Where Players Buy Games
When asked to name stores that sell games without prompting, players most often name PlayStation Store (16%), Steam (13%), and Xbox Store (12%).
Among stores not owned by platforms or publishers, only Amazon (8%) and reseller Loaded (formerly CDKeys, 1%) made the top 10. In Poland, players often name marketplaces such as G2A.COM (7%), Allegro (6%), and Eneba (5%). In Saudi Arabia, the local chain Jarir is mentioned most often (16%).
Over the past year, 49% of respondents bought something on PlayStation Store, 37% on Steam, and 30% on Xbox Store. Awareness of PlayStation Store converts into purchases best: 53% of those who know it bought something there (69% among PlayStation players).
Among resellers and marketplaces, Newzoo highlights Instant Gaming (44% aware, 4% bought), G2A.COM (39% aware, 3% bought), and Loaded (34% aware, 3% bought). The highest awareness-to-purchase conversion, though, belongs to Eneba (14%) and Kinguin (11%).
97% of respondents used official storefronts over the past year, and 94% bought something there. 24% used marketplaces (14% bought), and 16% used resellers (9% bought).
Among console-only players, 16% use marketplaces, and 9% use resellers. Newzoo attributes this to the closed ecosystems of PlayStation and Switch.
Marketplaces are used most in Poland (45%), Saudi Arabia (37%), and Germany (31%). In the US, the figure is 21%.
Official storefronts are trusted more: 64% are confident they'll get what they paid for (53% for resellers, 41% for marketplaces). 30% of marketplace users doubt purchase reliability (though 14% of official storefront buyers do too).
Small purchases, on the other hand, are more often made on marketplaces (39%) and with resellers (35%) than in official storefronts (20%).
On value for money, resellers lead (51%), marketplaces are second (45%), and official storefronts follow (43%). Marketplaces are more often associated with access to regional prices (30%) and rare keys (29%), while official storefronts are associated with a large library (43%).
