The EU is coming for F2P monetization
On November 11, the European Commission
presented its proposal for the Digital Fairness Act
. For games, it means showing purchase prices in real money, adding an option to turn off addictive design (like persistent prompts), and banning “gambling-like features for minors, such as pay-to-win and pay-to-progress.” The draft also covers loot box odds and switches paid items and currencies off for minors by default.
Fines for non-compliance can potentially reach 6% of worldwide annual turnover.
It’s not a law yet!
Here's how the process will look:
The European Parliament (most likely its consumer protection committee, IMCO) will appoint a rapporteur, draft a report, and vote on amendments, while the 27 member states agree on their own position in the Council.
Then all three institutions sit down for closed-door negotiations (trilogues) to agree on a final text, and that’s where most of the conversation will happen.
Once Parliament and Council formally adopt it, the law is published, and companies usually get a transition period before the rules kick in.
There’s no official timeline yet, but EU laws of this size typically take a
couple of years
to pass. The proposal will almost certainly change on the way. Still, Parliament has historically been the more consumer-protective of the EU institutions, so the DFA getting much softer is unlikely.
