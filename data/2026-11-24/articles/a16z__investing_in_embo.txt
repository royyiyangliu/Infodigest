Title   : Investing in Embo

Robots can already do remarkable things in demos: fold laundry, prepare food, and sort objects. But demos often hide how fragile those skills still are. Move an object, change the lighting, or introduce something unfamiliar, and the robot can struggle.
AI is now moving off the screen and into the physical world, and that requires models that understand how the physical world works. What kind of model will get us there remains an open question. Some teams are building vision-language-action models, while others are extending general-purpose language models to control robots.
Embo
’s conviction is that world models are the next generation of frontier AI: models that learn how the world works, enabling robots to anticipate the consequences of their actions, recover from mistakes, and adapt to unfamiliar situations.
For Danijar Hafner, world model agents have been a decade-long pursuit. His doctoral thesis, Embodied Intelligence Through World Models, explored the foundations of this approach. His work on Dreamer showed how an agent can learn to act by practicing inside a learned world model. Most recently, Dreamer 4 learned to obtain diamonds in Minecraft from previously collected data, training its behavior entirely through imagined experience. He’s now bringing that research to Embo, which is building the brain for the humanoid workforce.
To achieve general robot capabilities, the company is tackling two closely related problems: generalization and resilience. A robot trained in one kitchen needs to work in any other kitchen it has never seen before. And when an object slips or a drawer sticks, it needs to recover and keep going. World models allow robots to learn general knowledge and turn that knowledge into reliable skills.
Robotics has traditionally struggled to produce enough general data to train on. Learning from human demonstrations has driven significant progress, but we can’t demonstrate every task in every environment or every way it might go wrong. World models unlock a much larger source: internet video, which contains an enormous record of how objects move, how materials interact, and how people perform tasks. Embo’s approach starts here, training a world model on massive amounts of video and robot demonstrations so that robots learn general knowledge far beyond what could be collected on their hardware.
Then an agent practices skills inside that model, trying different actions across imagined scenarios and improving through reinforcement learning. The world model provides physical knowledge, and practice turns that knowledge into skills.
This creates a path for robot learning to scale with compute. An agent can learn from far more experience than we could practically collect on physical hardware, including the failures and unexpected situations that demonstrations rarely capture, so it practices recovering as well as succeeding. Video models have made extraordinary progress in modeling the physical world, and Embo aims to turn that progress into a foundation for robot skills. The team is building a unified multimodal model that combines internet-scale pretraining with reinforcement learning, both in imagination and in the real world. Early results have been remarkably promising.
Danijar has teamed up with Wilson Yan, whose work includes VideoGPT and years of collaboration with Danijar on world models. The broader team has pioneered research including Dreamer, VideoGPT, and Diffusion Forcing, bringing together experience from Google DeepMind, Meta Superintelligence Labs, NVIDIA Cosmos, Berkeley, and MIT. They’ve spent years developing the world models and learning algorithms this effort requires. Now they’re bringing them together at a much larger scale.
A robot that can enter a new environment and work reliably could transform how we manufacture goods, move products, and deliver services. Building the intelligence that makes this possible is an enormous technical undertaking, with the potential to make goods and services more abundant and affordable. We’re excited to lead Embo’s round and partner with Danijar, Wilson, and the team as they build the brain for that future.
